# Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic grading of knee osteoarthritis

Reference definitions for the five-point Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) scale used by the
KneeXpert radiographic pipeline. Written for this project because published research
articles cite the KL scale constantly but rarely restate its criteria plainly, which
leaves a retrieval corpus of literature unable to ground a statement as basic as what
grade 2 means.

## Grade 0 — Normal

No radiographic features of osteoarthritis. Joint space is preserved with no
osteophyte formation. There is no subchondral sclerosis and no abnormality of bone
contour. A grade 0 radiograph does not exclude soft-tissue pathology: cartilage
damage, meniscal tears and ligament injury are not visible on plain radiography and
require magnetic resonance imaging to assess.

## Grade 1 — Doubtful

Doubtful joint space narrowing, with possible early osteophytic lipping at the tibial
spines. No definite subchondral change is present. The boundary between grade 0 and
grade 1 is the least reproducible point on the scale, and inter-observer agreement is
lowest here. A grade 1 reading should be treated as uncertain rather than as evidence
of early disease, and correlated with symptoms before any management decision.

## Grade 2 — Mild

Definite osteophytes are identified, typically at the tibial plateau and the femoral
condyles. Joint space narrowing may be present, most often in the medial compartment.
Mild subchondral bone change may be visible but without established sclerosis. Grade 2
is the threshold conventionally used to define the presence of radiographic
osteoarthritis in epidemiological work.

## Grade 3 — Moderate

Definite joint space narrowing, usually medial. Osteophyte formation is present at
multiple margins, which may include the tibial plateau, the femoral condyles and the
patella. Subchondral sclerosis is detectable, characteristically in the medial tibial
plateau. Some deformity of bone contour may be apparent.

## Grade 4 — Severe

Marked joint space narrowing, which may extend to bone-on-bone contact in the medial
compartment. Large osteophytes are present at all compartment margins. Subchondral
sclerosis is established and subchondral cyst formation may be present. There is
definite deformity of bone contour, commonly flattening of the femoral condyle.

## Features the scale is built from

Four radiographic features determine a KL grade. **Osteophytes** are bony outgrowths at
the joint margins and are the earliest reliably-visible feature. **Joint space
narrowing** is the indirect radiographic signature of cartilage loss, since cartilage
itself is radiolucent. **Subchondral sclerosis** is increased bone density beneath the
articular surface, appearing later than osteophytosis. **Bone contour deformity** is
the latest feature and indicates structural collapse of the joint line.

## Reliability and interpretation limits

KL grading is a reading of structure, not of symptoms, and the correlation between
radiographic grade and reported pain is weak. Inter-observer variance is substantial,
concentrated at the grade 0 to grade 1 and grade 1 to grade 2 transitions. A grade
assigned from a single projection is less reliable than one assigned from a
weight-bearing view, because joint space width depends on load. Automated grading
systems exhibit their lowest accuracy at the same boundaries that challenge human
readers, so a borderline automated grade carries the uncertainty of both the model and
the scale.

Radiographic grade alone is not a sufficient basis for a surgical decision, and a
normal radiograph does not exclude a symptomatic soft-tissue injury.
